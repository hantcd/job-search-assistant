# Where the person is starting from, and every way in

Read at setup (chat 1 asks the situation first; chat 2 suggests role families
from it) and whenever the weekly check widens the search. Facts about schemes
change: check each one's own page before suggesting it, and give the link.

## The situation decides the defaults

Ask first, before reading anything, because advice built on the wrong
situation is wasted (a coach-by-AI story that went wrong started exactly
there: the advice assumed a job the person had already left). More than one
can apply.

| Situation | What changes |
|---|---|
| **In work, exploring** (a better job, or alternatives to learn about) | Confidential by default: LinkedIn's Open to Work for recruiters only, sharing of profile changes off, nobody at the current employer contacted about moving, chats framed as learning. Interview and chat times outside core hours (`booking.py slots --employed`). Alerts only for jobs that beat the current one: the person rates their current job on the same Career Value parts at setup (`current_job` in the scoring JSON; margin 5). Personal email and device, never the employer's. References: the current manager last, and only with the person's yes. Before accepting a counter-offer: does it fix the reasons they started looking? (job-offer). |
| **Not in work** | Urgency and income first: how many months before they need an income (optional, never written into documents). Non-linear routes below are suggested alongside the target roles, not instead of them. Free and paid schemes for people on a jobseeker's payment. Encouraging, never guilt-tripping; the streak counts small steps. |
| **Student or new graduate** | Graduate schemes and internships by deadline (job-scout's graduate sites), the open-to-any-degree routes below, and about half of what's asked is enough to apply (schemes hire on potential). Societies, part-time work and projects count as evidence (the experience interview asks about them). |
| **Returning after a break** (caring, health, travel, study) | Returnships and return-to-work programmes; the break explained in one honest line, never apologised for (job-cover's gap check); free courses for returners. |
| **Changing field** | job-switch: bridge roles, the quickest credible proof, a checked "why I'm switching" story. |

## Open to any background (a role family of its own, or part of several)

A job can belong to more than one role family. For a new graduate, or anyone
early on, suggest an "open to any background" family alongside the ones their
evidence fits, because many good first jobs ask for a degree, not a subject:
- **Consulting and the Big Four**: business, strategy and advisory streams
  (McKinsey, Accenture's business streams, Deloitte, PwC, KPMG, EY) take any
  degree; technology streams usually prefer STEM.
- **Banking and finance**: graduate programmes such as Barclays, NatWest and
  Santander's take any degree; Irish banks and insurers run similar schemes.
- **Public service**: the UK Civil Service Fast Stream, NHS management
  training, HM Treasury and the FCA take any degree; in Ireland, publicjobs.ie
  graduate and Administrative Officer competitions usually ask for a degree in
  any discipline (check each notice).
- **Retail and commercial management**: Aldi and Lidl's area manager schemes,
  M&S, John Lewis, P&G and Diageo's commercial schemes.
- **Sales and business development**: SDR and BDR roles hire on drive and
  communication from any background, and lead to account executive roles.
- **Recruitment, operations, customer success and support**: entry roles in
  growing companies that train people and promote from within.
- **Law without a law degree**: the GDL conversion (UK); in Ireland, the Law
  Society's FE-1 entrance exams are open to non-law graduates.

## Ways in for someone not in work (non-linear, all real experience)

Suggest the ones that fit the person's situation, place and target families,
each with what it leads to, time to start, cost and its link:
- **A paid bridge job while searching**: temping through agencies,
  part-time, seasonal or contract work; a contact-centre or support role at a
  company in the target field is often the way in.
- **Ireland**: the Work Placement Experience Programme (6 months, 30 hours a
  week, at least €369 a week, for people on a qualifying jobseeker's payment
  for 4+ months in the last year if under 30, 6+ months if 30 or over; apply
  on JobsIreland.ie or through Intreo); Springboard+ (free courses at Levels
  6-9 for people who are unemployed or returning, 2026 courses start August to
  October; springboardcourses.ie); apprenticeships, including office and
  professional ones such as accounting technician, insurance, recruitment and
  ICT (apprenticeship.ie); an Intreo Employment Personal Advisor.
- **UK**: Skills Bootcamps (free, up to 16 weeks, with a job interview offered
  on completion; check the local provider); apprenticeships at any age;
  Jobcentre Plus work coaches.
- **Elsewhere**: the country's public employment service and its free courses;
  EURES for jobs across the EU.
- **Skills-based volunteering**: a charity project, a board or trustee role
  (Boardmatch in Ireland), a community or sports club role with real
  responsibility. It goes on the CV as experience, with its results.
- **Freelance and small paid projects**: one client's real problem, solved and
  shown, beats a course certificate (job-portfolio; a value validation project).
- **Internships, returnships and fellowships**, including post-graduate ones.
- **Adjacent first roles** that lead where the person wants: support to
  product, SDR to account executive, coordinator to manager, analyst
  apprenticeship to analyst.
- **A smaller company**: more responsibility sooner, and the hiring manager
  reads the application (job-scout's `hidden.py`).

## How to suggest each path

For every role family or path suggested, in one line each: the match
(excellent, very good, good or fair) and exactly why; the gap and the quickest
credible way to close it (a credential, a project, a bridge role); time to
entry; pay at entry, mid and senior level, with sources (salary guides,
CSO/ONS/BLS); the five-year outlook, with sources (Ireland's National Skills
Bulletin, UK labour market projections, the US Occupational Outlook
Handbook); and how AI is changing the work, from the Anthropic Economic Index
(anthropic.com/economic-index; its data by occupation is public), so the
person builds the skills that are growing (judgement, client work, directing
AI) rather than the tasks being automated. Never more learning than the hours
a week the person said they have.

## How to coach (all situations)

Ask more than tell at first; questions that surface patterns the person might
miss. Small, concrete experiments before big decisions (one informational chat,
one weekend project, one short course). Call out, kindly, a contradiction
between what the person ranks first and what they say they want, or a plan
that plays it safe. Track the plan over weeks, revisit earlier statements and
celebrate progress (Seth Godin's coaching-prompt advice).
