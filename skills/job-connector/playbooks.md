# Industry playbooks (job-connector)

Read with SKILL.md when a role family falls in one of these fields. Each is a
specialist's method for students and new graduates, adapted to the pack's
rules (one-click approval, stop rules, never acting in LinkedIn). Ideas only;
none of their text is used. MASTER_PLAN.md records which playbook each role
family uses; the route and the stop rules still apply on top.

## Banking and finance (Brian DeChesare, Mergers & Inquisitions)

- Alumni first, then people at boutique firms; at large banks, only with
  strong internships behind you. Bankers live in email: short, specific
  emails, never long or general ones.
- Start 3-6 months before the firm's recruiting opens (openings calendar).
- A 15-20 minute call: mostly listening, about their path and the search.
- Then a **mini-ask** ("would you pass my CV to the team?" or "who else should
  I speak to?"), updates when something changes, and later the **real ask**:
  bringing the person into the firm's recruiting.
- Cold calls only to small boutiques, and only for students and new
  graduates. Claude never calls; it prepares a 30-second script.

## Consulting (Management Consulted)

- People you know, then alumni ("McKinsey" + school), then cold email to the
  firm's address format. Not LinkedIn messages.
- A consulting-ready CV before any outreach: consultants ask for it early.
- One email and at most one follow-up; ask for 10-15 minutes, with up to
  three specific questions, and end by asking for a referral.
- **2/2/2**: at most 2 consultants per office, within 2 weeks, at most 2
  contacts with each (pipeline.py contact-check enforces the team cap).
- A 3-5 sentence thank-you within 24 hours. Only about 20-25% of applicants
  reach interview, so the conversation is the edge.

## Tech and start-ups (Jerry Lee, Wonsulting)

- Per target company: day 1, a comment on the hiring manager's or
  recruiter's post and a connection request; day 2, a short email (under 5
  sentences, one real result, a 10-minute ask); day 5, a follow-up that adds
  something (a project, a reaction to company news); day 7, a call the person
  makes, if they want to; day 10, a peer on the team instead
  (`network.py plan`'s second wave).
- At small firms and with founders, send the finished sample of work, not an
  offer to do one (Alex Hormozi: "Don't ask how you can provide value. Do
  it.").

## Graduate schemes in Ireland and the UK (Sinéad Brady, UCC Careers, ISE, Prospects)

- Apply when about half of the requirements are met (internships, part-time work and coursework count): schemes hire for
  potential, not track record. job-cv's audit treats 60+ as worth tailoring
  for graduate schemes.
- Map experience (part-time jobs, societies, study abroad, managing
  deadlines) to the employer's strategy and values, with numbers.
- Expect fewer UK graduate vacancies in 2026 (ISE: down 7%) and more
  apprenticeships; insight days and spring weeks open doors early.
- AI for checking, not writing: applications that read alike are passed over.

## Any field (Katie Jolicoeur, Minnesota State University careers)

- Translate the major into the role's language; be ready to say how you use
  AI well; a referral matters more now that employers are filtering out fake
  applications.
